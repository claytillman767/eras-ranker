# Category 1 — Best Breakup Song

**Status:** Expanded top-50 (scored). Same rubric/criteria/guidance as the finalized top-25;
this run extends the ranked list to 50 so deeper cuts get fair consideration for the 26–50
range. Clay's next step: cut to 16 + seed.
**Hook:** "For when it's truly over."
**User guidance line (shown to voters in-app):** *"Which song best captures the feeling of
a relationship ending — the one you'd put on when it's truly over?"*

**Theme discipline test:** the song is ABOUT a romantic relationship ending or its
immediate aftermath. Out of scope (a neighbouring category owns it): songs that long to
KEEP/return to the person (longing), and spite aimed at a critic, rival, friend, or
reputation rather than the partner (→ a future Best Kiss-Off).

## Scoring criteria (weights sum to 100%)
| Criterion | Weight | What it measures |
|-----------|--------|------------------|
| Theme | 50% | How squarely the song is about a breakup — a relationship ending or its aftermath. Off-target spite (critic/rival/bully/reputation) and "want them back" longing score low. |
| Emotional Weight | 20% | How much THE BREAKUP itself lands (ache or anger, both count). Emotion from a non-breakup subject does NOT count, so this tracks down with Theme for off-theme songs. |
| Popularity | 30% | How well-known/singable — how likely a casual fan knows it cold and votes with conviction. Weighted up for launch so casual users aren't bogged down by unknowns. Never buys a spot for an off-theme song. |

Total = Theme×0.5 + Emotional Weight×0.2 + Popularity×0.3, rounded.

## The columns
- **App blurb** = what ships to voters in the app: a neutral, copyright-safe, ~1-sentence
  description of the song's situation. No lyrics, no judgment adjectives.
- **Lyric (internal)** = verbatim line from `taylor_swift_lyrics.txt`, kept only so Clay can
  verify the pick is grounded. This NEVER ships to the app (copyright).

## Notes on specific picks
- **Ranks 1–22** are the clean on-theme set (all 71+): the relationship is genuinely
  ending or its aftermath is the subject. The 26–50 expansion pulled several strong deep
  cuts into this band — **exile, So Long London, Death by a Thousand Cuts, The Black Dog,
  happiness, How Did It End?, Clean, loml, Maroon** are all squarely breakup songs that the
  original top-25 had room to omit only because Popularity capped their totals below the
  marquee hits.
- **Saddest overlap.** Several new entries (Death by a Thousand Cuts, happiness, loml,
  Maroon, The Black Dog, How Did It End?) sit on the Best-Breakup / Saddest seam — they ARE
  about a relationship ending (so they score on Theme here) but their tone is grief, not a
  defiant walk-away. They're the natural shared pool with a future Saddest bracket; flagged
  here only where the lean is pronounced.
- **#9 I Knew You Were Trouble.** — Clay's earlier rank-override slot; with Popularity at
  30% it earns its high total on merit, so the override is no longer load-bearing. Seed on
  merit unless Clay wants it higher for fame.
- **#23 Tell Me Why** — strong Theme, low Popularity (deep-ish cut). KEPT IN deliberately as
  a live test of how hard fans punish a great-fit song they don't know well.
- **Ranks 30–50** are mostly the off-target / longing / on-off band (rebound, reputation,
  friendship, rival, plea, wistful longing) and flagged BORDERLINE — natural cut territory,
  kept for Clay's discretion and as feeder material for a future Best Kiss-Off / Saddest.
